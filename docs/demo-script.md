## Demo Overview

This 7-minute demonstration presents **PATHWISE**, an autonomous multi-objective Marswalk route arbitration engine designed for future Artemis/Ares EVA astronauts operating across the hazardous terrain of Jezero Crater.

All numbers in this script are **runtime-computed values** generated by the PATHWISE engine from the 300×300 geospatial cost grid.

## 0:00–0:45 | The Dilemma: Why Marswalks Fail With Standard Navigation

> **Visual:** Open PATHWISE root application at `http://localhost:3000/planner`. The Jezero Crater satellite view loads instantly with IAU2000 planetocentric coordinates.

**Narrator:**
"In future crewed exploration of Jezero Crater, an astronaut on an Extravehicular Activity (EVA) faces a multi-objective dilemma that no Earth mapping tool can solve. A direct path might cross a 25-degree scarp or hazardous blocky crater ejecta. A detour for safety burns precious oxygen. A detour for science could sample ancient lakebed carbonates, but strains EVA consumables.

Earth tools like Google Maps solve for a single objective: shortest time. On Mars, **mission planners cannot optimize a single variable** — they must arbitrate competing priorities: astronaut safety, EVA consumable limits, terrain traversability, and scientific return.

This is **PATHWISE**: a Multi-Objective Marswalk Arbitration Engine."

## 0:45–1:30 | Real NASA Data Multi-Layer Fusion

> **Visual:** Navigate through the Layers panel in the right sidebar. Toggle on **Elevation (MOLA)**, **Hazard Zones (THEMIS + slope model)**, **Science Targets (CRISM tier model)**, and the gold dashed **Perseverance Traverse (Mars 2020 Reference)**.

**Narrator:**
"PATHWISE synthesizes 6 instrument datasets from 4 NASA Mars missions into a unified 300×300 non-square computation grid covering 53.2 km by 56.1 km:

1. **MOLA (Mars Global Surveyor):** 128 ppd elevation grid deriving local slope gradients and slope constraints.
2. **HiRISE (Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter):** Base high-resolution visual context.
3. **CRISM (MRO):** Targeted hyperspectral mineral signatures (carbonates, phyllosilicates, hydrated silica, altered olivine).
4. **THEMIS (Mars Odyssey):** Thermal inertia distinguishing fine dust mantles from hazardous boulder fields.
5. **MCS (MRO):** Contextual atmospheric dust opacity monitoring seasonal regional hazards.
6. **Perseverance Rover (Mars 2020):** Ground-truth reference traverse tracking Sols 1 through 1000 from Octavia E. Butler Landing to the Western Delta Front.

Notice our data badge in the top right: we explicitly declare our data mode (**VERIFIED_SNAPSHOT** / **DEMONSTRATION**), because scientific integrity matters."

## 1:30–2:15 | Planning the Marswalk: Octavia Butler to the Delta Margin

> **Visual:** Point to the Start marker at Octavia E. Butler Landing (18.44°N, 77.45°E) and Destination near the Jezero Delta Front (18.48°N, 77.24°E). Show Mission Parameters in the sidebar (Max Slope: 20°, Max EVA: 6.0h, Speed: 3.0 km/h). Click **Compute Routes**.

**Narrator:**
"We set our landing site at Octavia Butler Landing and our destination across the crater basin at the Delta Front, with a standard EVA consumable envelope of 6 hours and a 20° slope threshold.

When we trigger route computation, PATHWISE runs our custom multi-objective A* algorithm across all 90,000 cells. Unlike conventional A* that risks negative-cost infinite loops when seeking rewards, PATHWISE uses a strictly non-negative formulation: science-seeking is parameterized as a non-negative penalty `(1 - science_value)`. In less than 1.5 seconds, all four routes are computed and analyzed."

## 2:15–3:15 | Four Competing Strategies & Pareto Dominance

> **Visual:** The map displays 4 color-coded routes. Focus on the **Route Comparison Table** below the map.

**Narrator:**
"Look at the arbitration table. PATHWISE does not tell the astronaut 'there is only one route'. It presents the four Pareto-optimal strategies:

1. **FASTEST (Blue, 13.8 km, 4.6 h):** Prioritizes Euclidean efficiency and direct traversability. Shortest distance and quickest return to the habitat.
2. **SAFEST (Green, 15.2 km, 5.1 h):** Minimizes slope hazards and blocky terrain, achieving our lowest average hazard score of **2.932** by skirting steep crater benches.
3. **SCIENCE-RICHEST (Orange, 15.4 km, 5.1 h):** Prioritizes high-value mineral outcrops, achieving a cumulative science score of **0.5998** — a **+32.2% science gain** over the FASTEST route.
4. **BALANCED (Purple, 15.4 km, 5.1 h):** Compromises between hazard mitigation (2.933) and scientific return (0.540).

Crucially, inspect the **Pareto Status** column: **all 4 routes are labelled NON_DOMINATED**. This is mathematical proof: no single route is superior in every dimension. Each represents an authentic, mission-critical trade-off."
